# Publication Strategy

## Principles

Publish negative results. Share code as data terms require. Report to the matching guideline. Give African collaborators real authorship and lead roles, not acknowledgements alone.

## Recommended first paper

**Paper 1.** It needs no patient contact, uses data with strong labels, and adds African evidence. Scope it narrowly: one or two open models, calibration, subgroup analysis if metadata allow, and shortcut analysis. A candid statement that Naija-CXR is small, or that models degrade, would still be useful.

**Risks:** Naija-CXR terms and size are unknown; VinDr needs credentialing; code must be open.

## Possible venues

*Radiology: Artificial Intelligence*, *PLOS Digital Health*, *BMJ Health & Care Informatics*, *The Lancet Digital Health*, *Journal of Global Radiology*, and a preprint on medRxiv. Check scope, costs and waivers before submitting.

## Authorship

Use ICMJE criteria. Agree authors early, and include radiologists, African data owners where they contribute, and statistician. A clinician in training should expect a supervisor for first papers.

## Rules

- PhysioNet: cite the dataset, the paper and the platform [D19]. Share the code.
- Pre-register protocols where possible, such as OSF, or a trial registry for trials.
- Kenyan data publications follow the data agreement, and a health research publication may need authority's clearance.
- No patient-identifiable case reporting without consent and ethics review.
- State competing interests. A future product is one.
