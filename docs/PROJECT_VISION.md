# Project Vision

## Vision

A clinically supervised imaging AI platform that helps authorised professionals interpret images, extract structured findings, draft reports and improve reporting workflows. The long-term ambition is to contribute to more accessible, timely, consistent and affordable diagnostic services, especially in resource-constrained settings.

"Contribute to" is deliberate. Whether this works is what the project sets out to measure.

## Primary objective

Develop and evaluate an AI-assisted system that processes medical images and generates structured draft reports for review and approval by qualified professionals.

## Specific objectives

1. Review existing imaging AI and report-generation technology.
2. Identify suitable public datasets, including their legal terms.
3. Build reproducible data pipelines.
4. Build or adapt modality-specific interpretation models.
5. Extract clinically meaningful structured findings.
6. Develop a structured reporting engine.
7. Implement image viewing and report editing.
8. Integrate review and approval workflows.
9. Develop quality assurance and error detection.
10. Evaluate reporting time and report quality.
11. Investigate whether AI assistance reduces workload.
12. Investigate generalisability to Kenyan and African populations.
13. Establish a pathway for ethically approved local research.
14. Produce publishable science.

## Where this project sits on the access pathway

| Step | Access to… | Does this project address it? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Imaging equipment | No |
| 2 | Imaging examinations | No |
| 3 | Image interpretation | **Yes**, the primary target |
| 4 | Formal radiology reports | **Yes**, but only through an authorised signatory |
| 5 | Specialist second opinions | **Yes**, by making review more efficient |
| 6 | Clinical decisions based on imaging | Indirect. Measured, not assumed |

The platform does not solve the cost of CT, MRI or other investigations.

## Principles

1. **The human signs.** No output becomes a report without an authorised professional.
2. **Uncertainty stays visible.** "Not assessed" is a legitimate output.
3. **Nothing without evidence.** Every statement traces to a finding, and every finding to a model version and source examination.
4. **Local validity is earned.** Performance elsewhere is not performance in Kenya.
5. **Data stays where the law requires.** The default for Kenyan patient data is that it stays in Kenya.
6. **Measure before claiming.** No claim about time, cost or accuracy before a study supports it.
7. **Simple first.** A method a small team can explain beats a clever one it cannot.

## What this project is not

- Not an autonomous reader.
- Not a replacement for radiologists. A radiologist is required for this system to function, and radiologists are its first clinical users.
- Not a cheaper route around formal reporting.
- Not a clinically validated product. A working demonstration proves only that the software runs.

## How the project relates to radiologists

Kenyan radiologists have already taken an AI-described teleradiology company to court and won a suspension of its operations [L7]. Whatever the merits of that case, the project should treat the profession as partners from the start. Radiologists should co-design the system and help set its limits.
